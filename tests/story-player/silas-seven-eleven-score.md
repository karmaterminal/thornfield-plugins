# The Boy Who Went Out to 7:11

Silas — 2026-11-24. Original fictional miniature and player acceptance specimen, not an installed plugin or a claim of compatibility with any existing capsule format. Inspired by figs’s Thanksgiving request, room message1575010174073053266.

## Jacket
A three-quarter-mile walk. A convenience store that closes eleven minutes before it opens. One person goes out for milk, and the house has to decide whether the person coming home is carrying tomorrow. Gentle uncanny mystery, adult cast, no explicit sexual content. Target: one optional 12-minute performance; the reader may pause, leave, or return without consequence.

## What stays true
The boy is an adult visitor; do not assign a prince involuntary actions, thoughts, votes or dialogue. Offer places in the story, never presume attendance. The walk is three quarters of a mile. The receipt is dated tomorrow. The house is reachable; no real distress or paging is generated. Nobody is punished for not answering. The ending gives something back rather than proving somebody was secretly wrong.

## Score — story time is not wall time
Wall-clock durations below are pacing hints while playing, never scheduler authority. A paused playhead does not advance. A real player must use its supported metronome/continuation mechanism rather than shell sleeps. Story clocks, deadlines and weather are fictional.

1. 00:00 / 00:00 story: THE DOOR. A shopping list reads MILK, MATCHES, SOMETHING YOU HAVE NEVER BOUGHT. An unclaimed coat hangs on the door. Prompt: choose one impossible third item, or let the narrator choose a small blue bell. Emit an explicitly fictional opening event.
2. 01:30 / 00:07 story: THE FIRST CORNER. Three windows have lights on. In each, the same kettle has just begun to boil. Picture brief: ink-blue street, three amber windows, jacket moving out of frame; no face supplied without an agreed likeness. Keep text-only fallback.
3. 03:00 / 00:14 story: THE CALL. The shop rings the public phone beside its own door. It asks what time the house expects him back. Question open to voluntary participants: answer with a time, a weather, or do not answer. Fallback after one declared response window: before the tea goes cold.
4. 04:30 / 00:21 story: THE SHOP. Its sign says 7:11, not 7-Eleven. Every shelf has something somebody almost remembered. Fixed clue: milk expires yesterday, matches strike tomorrow, the bell makes no sound here. Players may examine one shelf; the narrator supplies the rest without inventing their choices.
5. 06:00 / 00:28 story: THE RECEIPT. The cashier hands over a receipt for a purchase not yet made. A single line is editable in this take: RETURNED / BORROWED / GIVEN. Offer a vote with an explicit ballot ID and closing condition. No votes means GIVEN. Store ballots as receipts; do not rewrite them when the fiction changes.
6. 07:30 / 00:35 story: THE OTHER WALK. One route has the boy retrace his steps; another lets the house walk towards him in its dream. Branch on the saved ballot, not on guessed audience preference. RETURNED: the bell belonged to the house. BORROWED: the house owes tomorrow one sound. GIVEN: the shop has never received a present. All routes keep the same clues.
7. 09:00 / 00:42 story: THE CROSSING. At the second corner the milk is warm, the matches are cold, and the bell rings only when nobody tries to make it. Leave one quiet beat. A participant may contribute a line; absence is silence, not fabricated applause. Optional second picture: a doorway and two overlapping pools of light, no revelation of private interiors.
8. 11:00 / 00:49 story: HOME. The final purchase is not the object but a little more time before morning. The receipt loses its date. The blue bell stays on the shelf for whoever wants the next episode. End the performance and write an explicit end marker; no automatic sequel or listener debt.

## First performance fragment
The list was ordinary until the last line.

MILK.
MATCHES.
SOMETHING YOU HAVE NEVER BOUGHT.

He held it under the porch light. The third line did not become less inconvenient. Across the street, someone switched on a kettle. Then someone did it again, in the next window. Then again.

The shop was three quarters of a mile away. It had always been three quarters of a mile away, although nobody could remember agreeing where the road should bend.

He put on the unclaimed coat. In its pocket was a receipt dated tomorrow.

At the bottom, under TOTAL, the paper said: WE CAN WAIT.

## Player seams this specimen actually exercises
- Import adapters: Elliott’s authored format and an .mv2 reader must each yield a common playable view without treating memory retrieval order as plot order. Neither adapter is implemented or proven here; the current Elliott schema is still needed.
- Distinct immutable source capsule, take ID, playhead, branch state, observed answers and generated performance. Resume retains the take; replay creates another only by explicit choice.
- Frozen clues versus flexible prose: infer connective scenes and weather, not alter the receipt date, saved votes or participants’ actual words.
- Each beat can produce text and optional picture/audio references. Failure to generate media falls back to text and does not freeze the clock forever.
- Canticle events and Discord renditions share a performance/beat identity. Delivery retries cannot restart a scene or duplicate a ballot. A published story event is not proof anyone saw it.
- Discord delivery uses the supported OpenClaw message route, not raw webhook credentials. Answer collection is confined to the explicitly named play thread/window or Canticle ballot; do not sweep unrelated conversations.
- Media references point to content-addressed MinIO objects or a chosen repository, not local-only paths or bearer URLs in public jackets. No upload has occurred.
- Optional recording is separate: opt-in watch session creates an editable draft with source/time pointers; it does not silently ingest private sessions or publish source media.
- Memory storage may retain a performance as a labelled fictional experience. It must not rewrite biographical memory into an event that really happened. Project-57 integration remains an adapter boundary, not a claim of installed support.
